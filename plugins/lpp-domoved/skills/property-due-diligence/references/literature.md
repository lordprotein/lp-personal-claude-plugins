# Methodology literature and sources

Marks: **[verified]** the conclusion or figure was confirmed in an abstract or secondary review during research; **[needs checking]** taken from memory, a partial source or a paid reference book, check against the original before relying on it firmly.

## International research

### Hedonic pricing

- **Rosen S. (1974). Hedonic Prices and Implicit Markets. Journal of Political Economy.** The price of housing is made up of implicit prices of characteristics. Implicit prices depend on the local market: weights from one market cannot be transferred to another. [verified]
- **Sirmans G.S., Macpherson D.A., Zietz E.N. (2005). The Composition of Hedonic Pricing Models. Journal of Real Estate Literature 13(1).** https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:rjelxx:v:13:y:2005:i:1:p:1-44. Building age is most often negative, about −1% per year or less; floor area is the main positive factor. [verified]
- **Sirmans et al. (2006). JREFE 33:215–240.** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11146-006-9983-5. Coefficients for area and age depend strongly on geography but are stable over time. [verified]
- **Benson et al. (1998).** Water view +8…+60%. [needs checking]
- Studies of Hong Kong and Singapore (Chau et al.): +0.5…+2% per floor in high-rises. [needs checking]

### Liquidity

- **Haurin D. (1988). The Duration of Marketing Time of Residential Housing. Real Estate Economics 16:396–410.** https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:reesec:v:16:y:1988:i:4:p:396-410. Atypical houses take longer to sell. [verified]
- **Genesove D., Mayer C. (2001). Loss Aversion and Seller Behavior. QJE.** https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:116:y:2001:i:4:p:1233-1260. Sellers unwilling to take a loss set asking prices 25–35% of the expected loss higher, obtain 3–18% more and sell more slowly. [verified]
- **Krainer J. (2001). A Theory of Liquidity in Residential Real Estate Markets. JUE.** Liquidity is procyclical. [needs checking]

### Location

- **Cortright J. (2009). Walking the Walk. CEOs for Cities.** https://planning.org/knowledgebase/resource/9182199/. +1 Walk Score point = $700–3000 to the price, effect in 13 of 15 US cities. [verified]
- **Black S. (1999). Do Better Schools Matter? QJE.** https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:114:y:1999:i:2:p:577-599. +5% in school test results = +2.1…+2.5% to the price (boundary discontinuity method). [verified]
- **Debrezion G., Pels E., Rietveld P. (2007). JREFE.** https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/the-impact-of-railway-stations-on-residential-and-commercial-prop/. Housing within ¼ mile of a station +4.2%; +2.3% for every 250 m closer. [verified]
- **Mohammad S. et al. (2013). Transportation Research A.** https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transa/v50y2013icp158-170.html. Large spread in station effects, publication bias. [verified]
- **Crompton J. (2001, 2005).** A passive park nearby: up to +20%, fading out by 150–600 m. [needs checking]
- **Brander L., Koetse M. (2011). Journal of Environmental Management.** https://www.pubmed.gov/21763064/. The effect of greenery grows with population density and transfers poorly between regions. [verified]
- **Nelson J. (1982); Bristow et al. (2015).** https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/what-do-hedonic-studies-of-the-costs-of-road-traffic-noise-nuisan/. Road noise discount 0.4–0.9% per dB (range 0.08–2.2). [verified]
- **Nelson J. (2004). Journal of Transport Economics and Policy.** https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpe/jtecpo/v38y2004i1p1-27.html. Aircraft noise about 0.5–0.7% per dB. [verified]
- **Des Rosiers F. (2002).** Power lines −2…−10%, the effect disappears beyond 100–200 m. [needs checking]
- **Ready R. (2010).** Landfills: about −13% nearby. [needs checking]

### Investment and forecasting

- **Himmelberg C., Mayer C., Sinai T. (2005). Assessing High House Prices. JEP.** User cost of ownership as a yardstick for price to rent. [needs checking]
- **Case K., Shiller R. (1989). The Efficiency of the Market for Single-Family Homes. AER.** https://www.nber.org/papers/w2506.pdf. The market has momentum: ¼–½ of the annual change repeats the following year; predictability for an individual house is low. [verified]
- **McDonald J., Osuji C. (1995). Regional Science and Urban Economics.** https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:regeco:v:25:y:1995:i:3:p:261-278. Land prices near a new line in Chicago rose 17% before opening. [verified]
- **McMillen D., McDonald J. (2004). Real Estate Economics.** https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:reesec:v:32:y:2004:i:3:p:463-486. Confirmation of the anticipation effect. [verified]
- **Gibbons S., Machin S. (2005). JUE.** https://core.ac.uk/download/94488.pdf. Jubilee Line extension: about +9.3 pp to the price. [needs checking]

### Books and standards

- **Geltner D., Miller N. Commercial Real Estate Analysis and Investments.** DCF, capitalization rates.
- **DiPasquale D., Wheaton W. (1996). Urban Economics and Real Estate Markets.** Four-quadrant model, price gradient from the centre.
- **Appraisal Institute. The Appraisal of Real Estate.** Sales comparison approach, order of adjustments, the net <15% and gross <25% rule.
- **RICS Red Book; IVS (IVSC).** Market value, assumptions, scope of inspection.
